## 3. Event extraction (±2 µs about 10.040718423769968 ms)

- `event_window.csv` — 1 509 raw samples of the original raw, all 13 columns, full precision.
- `event_json.json` — per-signal max/min/mean over the window, local-vs-global test, timestep statistics.

Measured: the event is the **global** maximum of `V(sense)`, not merely a local one —
0.5243575292413777 V at exactly the anchor time (|V| limit for this point is the LT3757A SENSE abs-max 0.3 V, already failed in R1).
`I(Lpower)` at the anchor = 1.4005 A, while `V(src)/0.062 Ω` = 9.035 A. These two are **not mutually exclusive**

could not separate them. Resolving that was the point of the three new runs.

## 5. What the runs establish

### 5.1 The 0.524 V `V(sense)` spike is not reproduced at the anchor by any run
At exactly 10.040718 ms, and with identical R1-like 200 ns gear settings, the runs show
`V(sense)` = 0.0035 V (gear200), 0.00064 V (gear100), 0.00060 V (trap200) and `I(DREC)` ≈ −2.4 µA / −46 µA / −466 µA.
The runs show a **very different switching phase** by then (see 5.3). So the event cannot be relocated by
pointing at a time; only its phenomenology can be compared.

### 5.2 SENSE peak magnitude reproduces; its fine structure does not
Global `V(sense)` peak over the common complete window:

| run | peak `V(sense)` | time | `I(RSRC)` peak | `I(DREC)` peak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| gear 200 ns | 0.52508 V | 10.00902 ms | 12.162 A | −10.760 A |
| gear 100 ns | 0.51953 V | 10.01004 ms | 12.007 A | −10.610 A |
| trap 200 ns | 0.52896 V | 10.05688 ms | 12.206 A | −10.794 A |

- SENSE peak spread **1.82 %**, `I(RSRC)` **1.63 %**, `I(DREC)` **1.70 %**, `I(CSNUB)`/`I(RSNUB)` **0.03 %**,
  `I(LPOWER)` **0.01 %**, `V(drain)` **0.03 %**, `V(hv)` small. R1's global SENSE peak (0.52436 V) is reproduced
  by gear200 to **0.14 %**.
- These pass the lab's 5 % diagnostic-reproducibility screen for peak magnitude.
- **But** the 10–12 A level is now known to be the **shunt branch** `I(RSRC)` (12.2 A at 0.757 V across 0.062 Ω),
  not inductor current (`I(LPOWER)` stays ≤ 1.65 A). The original "9.035 A source current" reading of
  `V(src)/0.062` is therefore an under-read of the same fast branch, consistent with a ~10 ns-scale spike.

### 5.3 Switching phase and sub-peak fidelity are strongly solver/step dependent
- Gate rising edges near the end: gear200 at 10 040.5 / 10 050.98 / 10 061.46 / 10 071.94 µs; trap200 at
  10 046.30 / 10 056.85 / 10 067.38 / 10 077.92 µs — a **≈5.9 µs phase offset**, i.e. more than half a switching
  period. Comparing the three runs (or R1) at "the same wall time" is meaningless here; only magnitudes and
  charge over a complete window are comparable.
- Peak spread **> 5 %** for `V(gate)` **14.3 %**, `I(RF)` **19.5 %**, `I(RGS)` **14.3 %**, `I(RNEG)` **14.1 %**
  (`metrics.json → solver_time_step_sensitivity`). The spread tracks the 5.9 µs phase offset, not a magnitude error.
- Peak-*time* differences reach 760 µs between gear200 and gear100 — i.e. the gear runs do **not** agree on
  *which cycle* is the worst, only on how big the worst is.
- Phase-shift-independent evidence for residual numerical sensitivity:
  - Gate ringing amplitude itself differs: `V(gate)` trough **−14.72 V (trap200)** vs **−12.61 V (gear100)**,
    a **14 %** difference in a quantity that is set by the RF/RNEG/RGS network and the controller output, not by
    phase. So the fastest transients are **not** converged at 100–200 ns max step.
  - Gear100 is **not** more conservative than gear200: it emits 13 706 "Heightened Def Con" reductions vs 475,
    and reaches essentially 5.7 µs earlier (its phase offset is closer to trap than to gear200). Refining the step
    changed the solver's behaviour qualitatively; it did not converge it.
  - Extremes still not settled: relative settling ~1.0e-17 s (machine-epsilon stepping) appear in all runs, and
    the minimum step is identical (`9.97e-18 s`), so sub-ns detail is dominated by solver step control, not physics.
- **Conclusion:** magnitudes of the slow/energy-carrying quantities are robust to ~2 %; the *peak-to-peak ringing
  amplitudes of gate-branch currents and the instant of occurrence* are not established to better than ~15 %
  and are phase-dependent. Any numeric limit check against these fast currents needs a phase-independent metric
  (e.g. maximum over a set of complete windows) and its own convergence study.

### 5.4 Source/drain KCL and charge are incomplete by construction
`I(XQ:LS)`, `I(XQ:LD)`, `I(XNEG:ddio)`, `I(XNEG:dgr)` are absent, so the node-`src` and drain-node KCL cannot be
closed. Writing the partial sum `I(RSRC) − I(RF) − I(RGS)` over the ±2 µs window
(`window_metrics.json → kcl_src_partial`), the **maximum residual is 0.356 A (gear200 and gear100) vs 0.052 A
(trap200)**. This residual *is* the unobserved MOS source-port + normal-clamp current; its **7× solver dependence**
proves the missing-branch magnitude is exactly the part the runs cannot resolve.
Charge over the ±2 µs window: `I(DREC)` = −8.2e-11 C (gear200), −1.0e-10 C (gear100), +3.8e-8 C (trap200).
The R1 raw gives `I(DREC)` +2.09e-6 C and `I(LPOWER)` +4.91e-6 C over the same window. The new runs capture **none**
of R1's recovery charge because R1's event is not present at that time in them. Hence **diode recovery charge is
not reproducible in this diagnostic set** and the R1 value stands unverified.
